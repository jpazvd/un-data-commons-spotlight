# Spotlight 04 · Maternal mortality (SDG 3.1.1): post captions

## LinkedIn

Getting world maternal mortality below 70 per 100,000 live births by 2030 means cutting it 18.2 a year.

Since 2000 it has fallen 5.7 a year, from 327.6 to 197.3 in 2023, so SDG target 3.1 needs 3.2 times the pace of the last 23 years. At that pace the world would be near 157.6 in 2030. The highest ratios in 2023 were in Nigeria (992.8), Chad (747.5), South Sudan (691.8), the Central African Republic (691.7), Liberia (627.7) and Somalia (562.6).

A statistician's note: these are MMEIG model estimates, all for 2023. I read the 23-year average with care: the pace has slowed since 2015, and the world series rose in 2020 and 2021, the COVID-19 years, before falling again. Where deaths are not registered with their cause, a maternal death can go uncounted, so the estimate leans on surveys, censuses and models.

Which countries have cut maternal mortality anywhere near 18.2 a year, and how did they do it?

Method: linear, deaths per 100,000 live births per year, 2000 to 2023 base.

Data: UN Maternal Mortality Estimation Inter-Agency Group (MMEIG), via the UN SDG Global Database (indicator 3.1.1) and the UN System Data Commons.
